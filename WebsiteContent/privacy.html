Privacy Policy

Effective date: [DD Month YYYY]

In short: LearnScrumble has no account and doesn't ask for your name, email or phone number. Your settings, progress and the words you've learned stay on your iPhone. To create new words and pictures, the app sends your job, your two languages and the words you've already practised to our server, which asks OpenAI — only after you agree to it in the app — and keeps new words and pictures in a common word library on Google Firebase, without anything that identifies you. The app signs in to our server with a random ID, not with your name or email. If you report a wrong picture, only the word and your job are sent. If the app crashes or something goes wrong, a short technical report is sent so we can fix the problem. Usage statistics are sent only if you switch them on. We don't show ads, don't track you across apps and don't sell data.

2. Data stored on your iPhone

This data is created by you while using the app. It stays on your device and is not sent to us.

WhatDetailsWhy
Your setup The language you speak, the language you learn, your job (from the list), speech speed, chosen voice per language, daily reminder on/off and time, whether you finished the setup and the "How to play" tutorial, when you agreed to sending your job to OpenAI, and whether usage statistics are on. To remember your choices between launches.
Learning progress For each word you've answered: the word's English name, the language you practised it in, how many times you were right and wrong, and when you last answered it — and the word itself: the job it belongs to, the word in both languages, its picture and the picture's link. To show your scores and progress, avoid repeating words, and show your words in "My words" and repeat them, also without internet.
Free-word counter How many of your 10 free words you've used. Kept in the iPhone's secure storage (Keychain). To give 10 free words once. It stays on your iPhone even after you delete the app, so the free words can't be taken again by reinstalling.
Days in a row The last day you played and how many days in a row you've played. To show your streak, and to skip the reminder on days you've already played.
Daily reminders The reminders planned for the next 7 days. They are scheduled by iOS on your iPhone; no server is involved. To remind you at your chosen time, only on days you haven't played.

You can delete your answers and saved words for one language with Settings → Progress → Reset progress, and all app data by deleting the app (except the free-word counter, see above). Unlocked professions are kept by Apple with your purchases and come back with Restore purchases. The data may also be included in your own iPhone backups.

Spoken words are produced by your iPhone's built-in text-to-speech, on the device.

For troubleshooting, the app also writes short technical messages to the iPhone's own system log. Your job is marked private there, so iOS hides it outside of development tools. These messages stay on the iPhone; only the last few steps, without your job or words, are included in a crash or error report (see section 3).

3. Data sent to other services

OpenAI — creating words and pictures

We ask first. Before any request is sent, the app asks you to agree: on the setup screen ("I agree"), or once in a separate question if you set up the app before this question existed. Without your agreement the app can't create words, so no game starts and nothing is sent. The same explanation stays visible in Settings → Legal and support.

When the app needs new words, it sends a request to our server (Google Cloud Functions for Firebase, in the United States), which sends OpenAI, L.L.C. (United States):

  • your job (as chosen from the list);
  • the language you speak and the language you learn;
  • the English names of words you've already practised in that language, so they aren't repeated;
  • for new pictures: a short description of the object to draw.

The app talks only to our server; OpenAI's key is kept on the server, never in the app. Pictures are downloaded straight from the word library (Google Cloud Storage).

OpenAI doesn't receive your name, your random sign-in ID or any device identifier; our server sends only the items listed above. OpenAI receives our server's address, not your iPhone's. According to OpenAI's policies, data sent through its API is not used to train its models by default and may be kept for a limited time (currently up to 30 days) to monitor abuse.

Google Firebase — the shared word library

Words and pictures are stored in a library shared by all LearnScrumble users, on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Cloud Storage), operated by Google LLC / Google Ireland Ltd. Only our server writes to it; the app itself can't change it. The server:

  • reads words that already exist (by their English name), so known words and pictures are reused instead of created again;
  • adds new words: the English name, the word in your two languages, and a link to the picture;
  • marks which jobs a word is used for, by the job's common English name (for example "cook"), so the same words and pictures can be offered to the next learner with that job instead of being created again;
  • saves new pictures (images of objects drawn by OpenAI, never photos of you).

Nothing that identifies you is stored with these words — only the English job names they belong to, shared by everyone with that job. When your iPhone downloads pictures or calls our server, Google receives your IP address and technical information (such as the app and SDK version and device type) needed to deliver the service. Data location: the United States (Cloud Firestore: Google's “nam5” United States multi-region; Cloud Storage: United States multi-region). See section 7 for how transfers outside the EU are protected.

Google Firebase — the list of jobs people learn for

To see which jobs are used most, our server counts them in a list on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore). When you start learning for a job (once per job on your iPhone), its English menu name (for example "electrician") is counted.

The list stores only the English job name, how many times it was chosen, and when it was first and last seen — nothing that identifies you or your iPhone.

Google Firebase and OpenAI — reporting a wrong picture

If a picture doesn't show its word, you can tap the flag on the picture and confirm. The app then sends our server the word's English name, your job's English name and the link of the picture you saw. The server asks OpenAI to describe the object again for your job and draw a new picture, which is sent to your iPhone and kept there with the word — only you see it. The report is added to a list of reported pictures (word, jobs, number of reports, the picture you saw, the new picture and its description, times), and we receive a daily email with the new reports. After checking, we may use the new picture in the shared word library for everyone. To count how many different people reported a word, the server keeps a one-way code made from your random sign-in ID (it can't be turned back into the ID); nothing else about you is stored with a report. To stop misuse, the server also counts how many pictures each random sign-in ID reported today (overwritten every day).

Google Firebase — random sign-in ID and daily report count

So that only the LearnScrumble app can use our server, the app signs in to Firebase Authentication (Google) anonymously, without any screen: Firebase gives the app a random ID. It isn't linked to your name, email, phone number or Apple ID. Firebase deletes each anonymous ID 30 days after it was created, and the app then gets a new one.

With that ID our server keeps one small record: today's date and how many pictures you've reported today, overwritten every day. It limits reports to protect the service from abuse; it doesn't contain your job, your words or your answers.

Apple and Firebase App Check — proving requests come from the real app

Each request to our server carries a short-lived token from Firebase App Check (Google). On a real iPhone, the token comes from Apple's App Attest, which confirms that the request comes from a genuine copy of LearnScrumble on a genuine Apple device. It contains no personal information and isn't used to identify you.

Firebase Crashlytics — crash and error reports

The app sends a report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google) the next time you open it if it crashed, or if something went wrong without a crash — for example when new words couldn't be loaded because there was no internet. A report contains:

  • where in the app's code the problem happened, the type of error (for example "no internet" or "server busy") and a short technical description of it from iOS or the server;
  • the language you speak and the language you learn;
  • the app's last technical steps before the problem, such as "round finished" or "10 words loaded" — counts and steps only;
  • the iPhone model, iOS version, app version, free memory and disk space, and whether the app was in the foreground;
  • a random installation ID created by Firebase, so repeated problems on the same iPhone are counted once. It isn't linked to your name, your Apple ID or any other identifier.

Reports don't contain your job, the words you practise or your answers. We use them only to find and fix bugs. Crashlytics keeps them for 90 days, after which Google starts removing them from its systems.

Google Analytics for Firebase — usage statistics (only if you switch it on)

"Share usage statistics to help improve the app" is off until you turn it on, on the setup screen or in Settings → Legal and support. You can turn it off again at any time. Only while it's on, the app sends Google Analytics for Firebase:

  • which parts of the app are used, for example "setup finished", "round finished", "review started", "free words used", "unlock screen shown", "profession unlocked" (with the product's name) or "picture reported", with simple details such as the number of review words or the type of error when words couldn't be loaded;
  • the language you speak and the language you learn (sent when you finish the setup);
  • which job from the menu you chose;
  • the iPhone model, iOS version, app version, app language and approximate country (from the IP address);
  • a random app-instance ID created by Firebase, so visits from the same iPhone can be counted. The app doesn't use the advertising identifier (IDFA).

The statistics are about how the app is used, not about who you are. They don't include your job, the words you practise or your answers. Analytics keeps them for 2 months.

Apple

  • Purchases: professions are unlocked with one-time purchases through Apple's App Store, and restored there. Apple processes your payment; we don't receive your payment details. The app only asks Apple which professions you own. See Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Notifications: if you allow them, daily reminders are local notifications scheduled on your iPhone; no notification server or push token is used.

4. What we don't collect

  • No account, name, email address or phone number. (The app's anonymous sign-in uses a random ID only, see section 3.)
  • No location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No usage statistics unless you switch them on (see section 3).
  • We don't sell or rent data to anyone.

5. Why we use data (legal bases)

  • To provide the app you asked for — creating words and pictures for your job and languages, remembering your settings and progress (performance of a contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • To keep the free words fair and the service working and secure — the free-word counter, the daily report count, the random sign-in ID and App Check (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • To fix wrong pictures you report (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). A report holds only a one-way code of your random sign-in ID, to count reporters.
  • To improve the job menu by counting jobs in English, without anything that identifies you (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • To build the shared word library so words and pictures are reused for all learners (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). It contains no personal data.
  • Reminders only if you allow notifications (consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) — you can switch them off at any time in the app or in iOS Settings.
  • Crash reports to keep the app working and fix bugs (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • Usage statistics only if you switch them on (consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) — you can switch them off at any time in Settings.

6. How long data is kept

  • On your iPhone: until you reset your progress or delete the app. The free-word counter stays in the Keychain until the iPhone is erased.
  • Shared word library: for as long as the service runs; it contains no personal data.
  • Random sign-in ID: deleted by Firebase 30 days after it was created. Daily report count on the server: today's date and the number of pictures reported only, overwritten every day.
  • Reported pictures: the list of reports, the pictures drawn for them and the replaced pictures for as long as the service runs; the one-way reporter codes are kept with them. The daily report emails are kept in our mailbox.
  • OpenAI: according to OpenAI's API data policy (see above).
  • Crash reports and usage statistics: for the periods given in section 3.
  • Emails to support: for as long as needed to answer you, then deleted after 12 months.

8. Your rights

If you are in the EU/EEA or the UK you have the right to access, correct, delete and restrict processing of your personal data, to object to processing based on legitimate interests, to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time. Because the app has no account and keeps your data on your iPhone, most of it is already under your control (see section 2). For anything else, contact us.

You can also complain to a data protection authority — in Poland the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO, uodo.gov.pl), or the authority where you live or work.
